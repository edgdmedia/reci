The path to justice has never been straight. It winds through centuries of struggle, marked by moments of triumph and tragedy, progress and setback. Understanding this timeline helps us see where we've been and illuminates where we must go.

From the abolition of slavery to the Civil Rights Act, from Brown versus Board of Education to the Black Lives Matter movement, each milestone represents countless individuals who refused to accept injustice. These weren't just historical events, they were human stories of pain, perseverance, and hope.

The 1950s and 60s brought the modern Civil Rights Movement into sharp focus. Rosa Parks' refusal to give up her seat, the Little Rock Nine's courage in integrating schools, the Freedom Riders' determination, and Doctor King's dream articulated on the steps of the Lincoln Memorial, these moments galvanized a nation.

But the work didn't end with legislation. The fight for voting rights, economic justice, educational equity, and an end to police brutality continues. Each generation must take up the mantle, adapting strategies while holding firm to the core principle: all people deserve dignity, equality, and justice.

Today's movements build on this foundation, using new tools and tactics while drawing inspiration from those who came before. The march continues, and we all have a role to play.
