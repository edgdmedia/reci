I remember the day we decided enough was enough. It wasn't a grand moment, it was quiet, deliberate, and filled with a courage I didn't know I possessed. We gathered in the church basement, voices hushed but determined. The air was thick with fear and hope in equal measure.

The march was scheduled for dawn. We knew the risks. We'd seen what happened to those who dared to speak up, to demand what was rightfully ours. But staying silent had become more dangerous than speaking out. Our children deserved better. Our community deserved dignity.

As we walked down those streets, arm in arm, I felt the weight of generations before us and the hope of generations to come. Every step was an act of defiance, every breath a declaration of our humanity. They could beat us, jail us, turn dogs and fire hoses on us, but they could not break our spirit.

The movement wasn't just about laws and legislation. It was about reclaiming our narrative, our identity, our right to exist fully and freely in this country we helped build. It was about looking our children in the eye and showing them that change is possible when people stand together.

Years later, when I see young people marching for justice, I see us. The struggle continues, but so does the resistance. So does the hope.
